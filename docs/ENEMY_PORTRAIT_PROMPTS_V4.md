## The loop, per portrait

1. Fresh image-generation chat. One ID per chat, one image per request — never a contact sheet.
2. Paste one complete block from §2.
3. Optionally attach an approved Ashfall portrait as **style reference only** — it supplies pixel scale, palette discipline, contrast, and outline treatment. Never its people, pose, clothing, or composition. Each block repeats this restriction so it survives copying into a fresh chat.
4. Corrections stay in the same chat, one at a time, using §4. After two failed edits, revise the brief instead of rerolling.
5. Save the selected large image to `art_sources/portraits/<id>_source.png`.
6. Normalize:

   ```powershell
   C:\Users\ASUS\Applications\Godot-4.7.2\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64.exe --headless --path . --script tools/prepare_portrait.gd -- art_sources/portraits/<id>_source.png assets/portraits/<id>.png 20 '#20272B'
   ```

7. Judge at 64×64 and 50×50, never only enlarged. Run the review pass in §6 of the design bible and compare the visible identities against this pack's fixed cast, not an older prompt or reference image.
8. Record generator, date, prompt version, edits, and approval in `docs/ASSET_PROVENANCE.md`.
9. Do not start the next ID until this one is approved.

Every prompt carries a **SIGNATURE DETAIL** line. That detail is the reason the portrait exists — if it is missing or invisible at 64×64, the image is a reject regardless of how good it otherwise looks.

## 1. Casting and detail rules

All human characters are adults, ages 23–64. Several women are deliberately beautiful, with distinct facial geometry, expressive eyes, memorable hair, and practical clothing. Their cheekbones, noses, mouths, and eye shapes remain attractive through visible hardship: dirt caught in a sweat track, an old healed scar, chapped skin, uneven hair ends, or a greasy flattened braid. Beauty must never cause a generator to clean, smooth, rejuvenate, or groom them. Preserve natural asymmetry, believable adult proportions, fully covered workwear, and no sexualized posing.

Every human has a numbered **WEAR AND EXPRESSION** line paired with their **CAST** line. Use both. Give each face one dominant survival mark and one smaller supporting cue, placed where a real action or exposure would leave them. Vary the marks: wind abrasion, sun spots, healed cuts, old heat damage, soot, salt, grease, and dull green tunnel residue. Do not give everyone the same diagonal cheek scar or random mud spots. Human skin stays intact; scars are old, flat, and dry. Dirt lies over the authored complexion and must not recolor a person into a different identity.

Hair keeps its assigned color and basic silhouette but looks self-cut, slept on, tangled, sweat-clumped, or repeatedly tied. Loose hair is short and controlled where the job demands it: hunters trap frayed tufts beneath their wraps, and guards have helmet-flattened clumps. The Citadel maintains its equipment, but even its people have sweat, road dust, pressure marks, and old scars. Covered hair stays covered.

Expressions are specific physical actions, not seven versions of a hard stare. Vary brow tension, eyelid opening, lip pressure, cheek tension, head angle, and shoulders to convey defiance, apprehension, skeptical amusement, annoyance, concentration, private sorrow, or restrained fear. Keep the assigned gaze direction; a smile, where specified, is small and closed-lipped. Masks carry emotion through the eyes and posture without being removed.

The cast below is fixed, not a menu of interchangeable traits. Do not transfer a character from another faction, swap hair colors, add gray hair to younger adults, or default to the same woman-and-older-man pairing. Complexion is only one part of identity: skull shape, nose, jaw, brow, hairstyle, build, and collar must differ too.

At 64×64, prioritize **head shape → hair mass or covering → clothing color block → signature detail → expression and dominant wear mark**. Keep the main scar or stain to one broad stepped cluster, preserve clear eye and nose planes, and show messy hair as two or three uneven clumps. Eye colors and smaller details guide the larger source; never add tiny pixels or exceed 20 colors just to display every iris, freckle, or stitch. Match skin and hair colors with shared palette ramps rather than flattening everyone to the same brown or gray. Describe only head, shoulders, and visible upper chest; no off-frame boots or full-body fashion details.

### 1 · `enemy_stalker` — Ash Stalker (existing portrait; regeneration brief)

HEAD AND FACE: An elongated narrow bone-ash skull with a high uneven crown, flattened sealed eye ridges, shallow cheek hollows, and two small vibration pits along each cheek. There are no visible eyeballs, pupils, lashes, eyebrows, or open eye sockets. A low blunt nasal ridge has two small recessed nostril marks; the compact jaw tapers into a short chin. Thin muted gray-violet lips surround one small dark oval mouth opening, with no visible teeth, fangs, tongue, or saliva. Fine radial skin folds gather around the permanently sealed eye ridges, broad enough to read as healed creasing without appearing to be stitches or wounds.
NECK, SHOULDERS, AND HAND: A long flexible throat enters from the lower-left, with five or six broad transverse skin folds rather than exposed ribs. The viewer-left shoulder sits lower; the viewer-right shoulder is raised into the neck, joined by believable tendons under intact skin. One slender hand enters from the lower-right and curls inward around the mouth's negative space: exactly five elongated fingers, thumb opposed to the other four, with blunt dark nail tips and credible knuckle joints. Keep the fingers separated in stepped silhouette; the other hand stays out of frame. Matte ash skin with violet-gray joint shadows and broad parchment highlights; no pores or scattered blemishes.
IDENTITY LOCK: Preserve one sealed-eyed listening creature with a narrow skull, ribbed throat, and one five-fingered hooked hand. An attached style reference supplies pixel treatment only, never its people, anatomy, pose, clothing, or composition. For a limited edit of an existing portrait, use an identity-preserving edit brief instead of regenerating.
SIGNATURE DETAIL: Radial healed creasing over the sealed eye ridges makes the eyes appear deliberately closed long ago. Keep this as a few visible stepped folds at 64x64, not fine realistic scar texture.
COMPOSITION: Claustrophobic off-balance close-up filling about 88% of the square. Oversized head presses into the upper-right, long throat enters lower-left, and the hooked hand rises lower-right, framing a crescent of empty background beside the small mouth. No full body, extra hand, or centered symmetry.
STYLE: Handmade cinematic 16-bit pixel art on a true 64x64 logical grid. 16-20 colors, large deliberate anatomical clusters, hard stepped edges, selective dark-teal outlines, restrained dithering, broad matte skin planes, one apparent pixel size. Smaller details must not become single-pixel noise.
PALETTE: Near-black teal-charcoal, bone ash, violet-gray shadow, muted parchment, and one restrained steel-blue Underrail rim light.
BACKGROUND: One perfectly flat, fully opaque #20272B field, identical in every corner and between the fingers, with no cast shadow or tonal variation.
THUMBNAIL TEST: At 50x50, sealed eye ridges, a tiny whispering mouth, a folded long throat, and an inward hooked hand form one distinct off-balance silhouette.
AVOID: Extra creatures, human companions, visible eyeballs, open sockets, hair, clothing, alien-gray stereotypes, zombie decay, vampire fangs, horns, extra fingers, exposed bones or organs, wounds, blood, gore, victims, tunnel scenery, glow, fog, vignette, gradient, border, smooth shading, anti-aliasing, text, logo, or watermark.
