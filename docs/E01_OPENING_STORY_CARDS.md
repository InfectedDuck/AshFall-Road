# E01 — Opening story cards

Status: **design and editorial review complete; content and opening selection are E02 work.** These cards describe the expanded build's three candidate first encounters. They use the current effective scenes in `data/narrative_overrides_v1.json`, the event contracts in `data/events.json`, and Channel Nine's source patches in `data/living_road_events.json`. They do not activate the expanded default or change a save.

The first paragraph of each scene should state a practical need. Reach the first choice after about 100–160 words: the current effective setups are 143 words for Channel Nine, 159 for Market, and 93 for Cache. Channel Nine and Market use the `substantial` 110–180 word setup band; Cache fits its existing `compact` 60–100 word band. The three dilemmas ask different questions: **will I let someone hear me, what can I trade without lying, and which part of a shared cache can I safely take?** Include a short quiet beat after the decision. Do not turn any of them into a verdict on the survivor's character.

## Channel Nine — `outskirts_child_radio`

**Opening need → changed relationship → decision → resolution.** A child's repeated request for Talia plays from a radio while a live carrier remains under the loop. The survivor can reveal their voice to an unknown listener, find a bearing without contact, or leave the message available to the next traveler. At `lr_blue_van`, Talia can recognize a successful speaker, answer someone whose call went unreturned, use a traced bearing without pretending they met, or respond to a failed trace. The final family arrangement remains a decision made with the Vales, not an automatic rescue for any radio history. The repeated object is the **open handset**: at first a risk of being overheard, later evidence of what each person actually heard.

The current setup's live breathing and rooftop aerials already make both active intentions legible. Keep the possibility that the breathing is ambiguous; do not assert that Talia was listening when the call failed. A helpful speaker should give landmarks that Talia confirms on her end, as the existing successful passage does.

## Market Under Tarps — `outskirts_market`

**Opening need → changed relationship → decision → resolution.** The mask-maker needs a trustworthy account of the road from which the survivor arrived; the scale boy needs water and crates moved; the survivor needs treatment or a reliable tool. Sharing observed facts can buy medicine, spotting a mispriced scanner can buy competence, and labor can buy one of two weapons at a known fatigue cost. At exit the traders' stock, the work pile, or the survivor's carried gear has changed. The recurring object is the **kitchen scale**: it measures scrap, but the traders also measure whether a story or an afternoon of work is useful.

The current setup has strong physical details and distinct work routes. Targeted correction: the survivor has not visited the eastern underpass when this is the first event. The mask-maker should ask about the **approach from the west**; place one or two visible, checkable facts from that approach in the setup, then let her test those same facts in the success passage. Do not replace the error with another unvisited landmark. Her reason to ask is practical: where to send a cart or move exposed stock, not a request for a grand moral judgment. The market is already at its four-choice cap.

## Municipal Cache — `outskirts_cache`

**Opening need → changed relationship → decision → resolution.** The survivor needs food, water, medicine, or protection. A civil-defense locker offers uncertain provisions behind a failing lock; its outside hook offers a certain buckler. Forcing it risks injury and scars the access mechanism, decoding offers medicine without tearing the lock, and taking the shield leaves the contents unknown. There is no present person whose trust changes. The repeated object is the **multilingual instruction plate**: on a technical success it makes access possible; on failure it records only a wrong turn; if the survivor leaves, it stays available for someone else to read.

Keep this scene compact. Its current 93-word setup already fits; make “communal emergency locker,” the deteriorated bracket, the partly surviving instructions, and the exposed buckler visible before choosing. Do not describe exact unseen contents in the setup. The current body and outcomes are close; revisions are limited to factual claims and choice clarity.

**Outcome copy direction.** Keep the force success's “scarred but open” result and the failure's bleeding palm. Technical success may describe what is found *after opening*; remove the invented clerk who “never reached” the shelter. Technical failure should say only that this locker is now inaccessible. Replace the buckler outcome's “medicines and food for another traveler” with “whatever remains behind the door is still sealed.” The story cannot know another traveler will use it.
