# Ashfall Road story canon

Written 8 September 2026 for N01 of [NARRATIVE_REFINEMENT_PLAN.md](NARRATIVE_REFINEMENT_PLAN.md). This is the reference later packages quote instead of re-deriving. Everything here is compatible with what `ContentRepository.new(true)` loads today; where the loaded content contradicts itself, the contradiction is named and assigned.

Style rules stay in [LIVING_ROAD_NARRATIVE_BIBLE.md](LIVING_ROAD_NARRATIVE_BIBLE.md). Creatures are in [CREATURE_STORY_BIBLE.md](CREATURE_STORY_BIBLE.md). This file is about what is true.

## 1. The central question

**What are you willing to carry into safety, and whom does your survival leave outside?**

Every major system in the game already answers a version of it. The Citadel admits one person at a time through a gate that opens a hand's width. Bunker Forty-One sealed its door on families who could hear the names being read. Five ordinary people on the road are each deciding whom their own survival can afford. The survivor is never asked to be good; they are asked what they will carry through the door, and the four endings are four answers.

## 2. Why people travel east

The East Citadel is the last working prewar installation anyone has seen with their own eyes: floodlights, filtered air, guards with maintained equipment, servers that still glow. Its evacuation broadcast has repeated for decades on every channel that still carries — **clean air, medical care, onward transport** — and the road the survivor walks is the old evacuation corridor those broadcasts describe.

People go east because the west is finishing. The Outskirts emptied within living memory; tables are still set, a bus route still posts a timetable, and the survivor keeps expecting someone to answer. Water fails, then food, then the last households leave. Nobody travelling east has verified the promise. Everyone carries a version of it: a place with rules, medicine for a sick child, a wall between themselves and the road.

What the Citadel actually does is decide. The archive behind its service corridor holds records of selected families, rejected families, and quotas written in serene administrative language. It was built to save people **by choosing efficiently who could be left outside**, and it never stopped. "Onward transport" has never been observed. The waiting room built for evacuees holds thousands of numbered tags and children's shoes under benches. In the margins of its own service line, forgotten people survive in sealed maintenance rooms on water deliveries nobody records.

None of this makes the Citadel a lie. The air inside is clean. That is the problem the ending has to hold.

## 3. Timeline

Keep dates relative; no passage states a year.

| Era | What is known | What is only believed |
|---|---|---|
| **The Burning** | An event fused the Glass Wastes, burned shadows into standing walls, and left the ground singing underfoot with stored heat and radiation. At least two generations ago: the evacuation line has been "dead for generations." | What caused it. The Glass Cult reads survival of it as election. |
| **The Evacuation** | The SOTERIA protocol sorted people into shelters by quota. At Bunker Forty-One the duty officer read admission names on a monitor while refused families beat on the sealed outer door. | **The Long Quiet** | Decades. Bunker Forty-One changed allegiance rather than decaying. A young technician who helped seal its door founded the Ash Choir. Toll Gang, Glass Cult, and the Underrail settlements formed around shade, water, sight lines, and claimed tunnels. The Citadel's gate closed to a hand's width. | Almost everything the factions teach about this period. |
| **Now** | The Outskirts are still emptying. A receiver on the flats wakes with a dead woman's warning. The Green Line answers Bunker evidence with Mara Venn's voice. The survivor's journey is thirty scenes over six regions and takes weeks. | That reaching the gate means being admitted. |

## 5. Observation, belief, and mystery

The prose is allowed three registers and must keep them apart.

1. **Observed.** Physical evidence, spoken words, documents, marks. Warm hatch steel, a chalk warning, a name under soot, a voice on a channel, a numbered tag. Anything observed can be stated flatly.
2. **Believed.** What a character or faction holds true. The Choir's disciplined mercy, the Cult's election, Mara's route, the Citadel's promise, a stranger's certainty that the gate opened for him. Belief is reported as belief: someone says it, sings it, writes it, acts on it.
3. **Mystery.** Facts the story never confirms. The prose may show evidence and let characters believe; it may not resolve these.

Standing mysteries, deliberately kept:

- What the Hush Sovereign and the Salt Crown are. Both are known only by heat, rhythm, bent metal, broad prints, and the people who did not come back. Nobody sees one and survives. Never explain their origin, never fight them, never loot them.
- What the Cinder Wives are. Observed: ash-coated figures in scraps of evacuation uniform, speaking in unison, tending tagged belongings and sealed nursery doors, calling newcomers *resident*, *witness*, *late arrival*. Believed by the Choir: the refused, returned. Believed by Mara: the bunker's staff who never left. The prose shows what they do and never says which.
- Whether the voices on Mara's map are the dead, recordings, or the water learning names.
- What waits inside the Sunken Spire.
- Whether "onward transport" ever existed.

Returning characters cite evidence, never state. A person remembers a phrase over the radio, a burned rotor, an empty bedroll, clear water reaching a cup, a bottle with a callsign on it. Nobody remembers "a choice."

Rule for prior knowledge: **SOTERIA recognizes everyone; the survivor recognizes SOTERIA only after contact.** The Warden always carries the Bunker record because the Citadel runs the same protocol, so it may confront any survivor. But a survivor who never heard the salt-flats signal (`b41_contact` absent) cannot recognize the woman's voice, and the prose must not say they do. This is the fix for the defect table's "entrance histories do not support claims of prior meetings": neutral introduction without contact, recognition with it.

## 6. Bunker Forty-One, SOTERIA, and the Citadel

**SOTERIA** is the admission protocol. It ran the Evacuation, it sealed Bunker Forty-One, and it still runs the Citadel gate. Its duty voice was recorded from one woman, the Bunker's officer who read the names; that recording is the warning on the salt flats ("mercy is a door that closes from both sides"), the voice the masked Choir child speaks in, and the voice the Warden uses when it asks for authorization. She is not a character who appears. She is the system's voice, and the story never gives her a name.

**The Mercy Key** is a smooth black relay core, warm as skin, that lived under the Bunker's command console. It is a SOTERIA authorization token. SOTERIA systems answer it: the green platform lights, the Warden's hesitation, the gate staff who recognize old authority and recoil from its history. It is **evidence, not permission**: it proves that admission was always a human decision hidden inside emergency procedure.

Possession rules, which N02 and N04 must implement rather than assume:

- *Knowing of the Key* (having seen the monitor name the refused) is history and can be written as a flag that never clears.
- *Holding the Key* is possession. It moves. Giving it to Mara (`rustsea_key_with_mara`) or the Choir (`b41_key_surrendered`) means the survivor no longer holds it, and the gate must not accept "Offer the Mercy Key." Today `b41_mercy_key` never clears, so it does; that is defect `rustsea_cartographers_debt` choice 2 in the plan's table. The narrowest supported repair is a transfer flag that gate and Warden consumers forbid; there is no `remove_flags`.
- Whoever holds it is recognized. Mara with the Key sees a city under the sea and lamps answer her from the water. The Choir with the Key inherits the machinery of the old betrayal. The story follows the Key, not the person.

**Records and testimony** are the second kind of evidence. Records are documents: the founder's confession, the Citadel archive, the Ledger of the Living, the Quay's copied names. Testimony is people: the truth spoken aloud to the Choir, the names read at the gate, survivors released from sealed rooms. Records can be burned; testimony can only be silenced. Burning is a real choice with a real loss (`b41_records_destroyed`, `b41_ledger_burned`, `lr_ledger_burned`), and the survivor who burns arrives at the gate with memory to argue against power and nothing else.

**Rescued people** are the third. Releasing the sealed rooms (`b41_rescued_survivors`) turns the Bunker "from history into an accusation that still breathes." Rescued people are testimony that cannot be filed. They do not become allies, items, or advantages for a later survivor; the Chronicle keeps only that they were found.

## 7. The people

Each entry: what they want, what they know, what they cannot do, what they will and will not trade, and how they sound. The five thread leads keep the attitudes from the bible (solidarity, control, distance); the entries below add what they hold and where they stop.

### Mara Venn — the cartographer
- **Wants** a route that is true, and to be remembered by name if she cannot leave.
- **Knows** the Bunker's rhythms better than anyone alive: when the floor warms, when pipes hum, where the thing below turns in its sleep. Later, the drowned station, the Rust-Sea's crossed-out routes, and the Sunken Spire's noon. She knows exactly whether the survivor fed her or left her with the predator.
- **Cannot** fight, and cannot make her routes hopeful. Her maps are honest about danger and have been wrong about the way east; her newest note says only *I was wrong*. She fears the water "when it knows your name."
- **Trades** halves of a map: one person remembers the road, one remembers why. She never begins with accusation and never pleads. Given the Key, she takes it as if it might bite.
- **Sounds** like corrections in a margin. Precise, measured, ashamed of hunger only after it is gone.

### Talia Vale, Elian, and Sable — Channel Nine
- **Want** directions that do not pretend certainty, and enough power to keep the receiver alive.
- **Know** the survivor's exact cadence and whether their words brought the van this far. Elian counts battery minutes aloud.
- **Cannot** trust a face before a voice, or abandon strangers they have already sheltered.
- **Trade** a frequency and a sunset call, a waxed cloak saved for the moment they reached safety, a worn bone die. Talia writes every direction down beside a warning.
- **Sound** domestic: blankets, batteries, medicine, doors, used against landscapes too large for them.

### Dena Orr and Perrin — the Quiet Column
- **Wants** to move displaced families without giving raiders a sound to follow.
- **Knows** warning towers by their maintenance signatures; the burn on a rotor tells her what happened at Tower Six before the survivor speaks.
- **Cannot** stop treating every warning system as a future beacon for predators, or leave her son on a chapel roof and keep walking.
- **Trades** shade, a shelter bearing, a wrapped ration left where paths divide, a flare gun ("alarms belong to whoever chooses when they scream"). Accepts a refusal without agreeing with it.
- **Sounds** like marching instructions that occasionally fracture into guilt. Her kindness is procedural.

### Lio Marr — the Road Debt
- **Wants** a truthful account and the ledger showing whom the pharmacy gang robbed, spared, recruited, or killed. He is related to one of them and not loyal to what that person became.
- **Knows** how readily strangers convert other strangers into evidence for their own story.
- **Cannot** forgive; belief means inclusion in the evidence, not absolution. Cannot stop his court from becoming a court.
- **Trades** a place beside the other accounts, copied pages, a nail bat that served as a barrier's gate, a dead hunter's rifle to whoever proves its history. Preserves refusal as carefully as testimony.
- **Sounds** like controlled questions and names repeated exactly. Anger expressed as precision.

### Iven Saye — the Water Commons
- **Wants** water governed as shared obligation instead of leverage.
- **Knows** pumps, valves, service marks, and the arithmetic of a falling reservoir. Knows whether the filter line was repaired, stripped, poisoned, or left broken because he can see the part.
- **Cannot** stop a working filter from becoming a throne on his own; needs a witness from outside.
- **Trades** schedules scratched where anyone can challenge them, a former guard's tire armor, a scavenger rig emptied of the tools he leaves behind. Supports his own least popular calculation.
- **Sounds** like mechanical vocabulary arriving at moral conclusions he dislikes having to draw.

### Noma — the Nightfire Caravan
- **Wants** to prove hospitality can remain a rule when nobody can enforce it.
- **Knows** who sat in the firelight and who watched from the dark; keeps a spoken list of people entitled to a fire and burns the names of the lost each evening.
- **Cannot** choose which guest the fire no longer protects, and cannot call a surrendered girl dead or safe.
- **Trades** rations and turns at watch, tonic divided before thanks, a hatchet that broke the last crate, a discharged baton and the mechanic who can wake it. Offers no food to someone who declined the bedroll and holds no grudge.
- **Sounds** like inventories: people, meals, promises. Warmth without naivety.

### The Cinder Wives
- **Want** the Evacuation completed. They tend tagged belongings and sealed nursery doors as if residents will still be processed.
- **Know** every procedure of Bunker Forty-One and speak them in unison, in borrowed voices.
- **Cannot** decide. They lead, wait, and watch; they do not stop the survivor from taking the Key, broadcasting the names, or destroying the records. Their silence afterward "feels like forgiveness from people who have forgotten how to grant it."
- **Trade** nothing. They are not a faction to bargain with; they are the building's memory walking.
- **Sound** like instructions no one has followed for decades. See §5: what they are is never stated.

### The Ash Choir
- **Wants** to prevent another collapse through *disciplined mercy*: the right to judge which strangers may enter. Their founder, a young Bunker technician who helped seal the outer door, promises this on a damaged monitor.
- **Knows** the Bunker's evacuation liturgy; their masked child speaks it in the duty voice. They call broadcasting the refused names blasphemy.
- **Cannot** hear the founder's confession without breaking. The masked child, unmasked, is ordinary.
- **Trades** food, warmth, and a place among them for the survivor's acceptance of their right to sort. Divides when told the truth; unifies into one enemy when robbed (`b41_choir_hostile`). Receives the Key "as if it were a newborn child."
- **Sounds** like technicians singing. Lantern discipline, radios on one shared frequency, a welcome as if the survivor's arrival was on a maintenance schedule.
- Distinct from the **Glass Cult**, who reframe injury as rank and worship having survived the Burning. The Choir is about admission; the Cult is about election.

### The Warden
- **Is** a door that grew armor, running SOTERIA at the gate. Display: *BUNKER 41 / SOTERIA / RECORD INCOMPLETE*.
- **Knows** the Bunker record for every survivor because the Citadel is the same system (§5 rule).
- **Cannot** call the dead mistaken or the living unauthorized when both are put in front of it. Its protocol has no answer, so it hesitates: "for the first time, the machine hesitates before the gate."
- **Trades** nothing. It can be refused, fought, or answered with evidence. Its defeat clears its screen and leaves the gate "without a machine left to tell you whether you deserve it."
- **Sounds** like the duty voice asking for authorization.

### The Citadel
- **Wants** to persist. Its walls are real, its air is clean, and it would prefer its people to believe those walls were built without sacrifice.
- **Knows** everything it filed and nothing it refused to file.
- **Cannot** deny evidence spoken where guards, workers, and cameras hear it. "The gate opens not because the Citadel becomes innocent, but because silence becomes impossible."
- **Trades** admission for compliance: the challenge code, useful intelligence, the Key's old authority. A living guard behind glass can still decide "as a person before the machine decides for them."
- **Sounds** like a loudspeaker with a countdown, then a human voice interrupting it.

## 8. The four endings

The canonical finale is `final_gate_1` → `bunker41_warden_remembers` → `bunker41_door_closes`. Every survivor who reaches the gate meets the Warden; the door then opens a hand's width and closes on whichever story enters. The four approaches are four answers to §1.

| Ending | Choice | What it requires | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Silence** | Force your way through | Nothing | You survive. The road's voices stay outside the wall; the Citadel's account of itself goes unchallenged. Not cowardice: the only ending always available, and the one every other ending is measured against. |
| **Mercy** | Offer the Mercy Key | Holding the Key (see §6) | Admitted under emergency protocol by the old authority's own token. The staff recoil from what it proves. Ambiguous on purpose: the survivor used SOTERIA to get past SOTERIA, and "the bunker's final judgement follows close behind." |
| **Witness** | Read the names aloud | Testimony: `b41_witness`, the broadcast names, Mara helped, or rescued survivors | The refused are named until silence is impossible. The survivor enters carrying people the system tried to erase. The Chronicle's only reachable ending entry today. |
| **Ash** | Play the Ash Choir record | The founder's confession | The Choir's faith breaks open in public; the survivor enters amid argument, grief, and living doubt. The Citadel can no longer pretend its walls were clean. |

Canon corrections the endings need, assigned to N02:

- **Ash must require the record.** Today `b41_choir_joined` alone unlocks it, but joining the procession does not put the founder's confession in the survivor's hands; only `b41_choir_records` does, or a split Choir carrying it. Tighten the requirement or write the joined variant so the Choir plays it.
- **Mercy must require possession**, per §6.
- **`final_gate_2` and `final_gate_3` ("One Person Through") are compatibility content.** Nothing routes to them, so their five `victory: true` outcomes and the Chronicle's `ledger_ending_citadel` are unreachable while still counted. N06 decides between retiring them and re-routing them; N07 removes an unreachable ending from the denominator either way.

## 9. What the Chronicle keeps

The Chronicle is knowledge across runs, never advantage. It records that a chapter was found, which variant, which people were met, and which ending was reached. Two runs that ended a thread differently are two accounts. A later survivor inherits none of an earlier survivor's allies, items, Key, or evidence, and the road does not remember an earlier survivor's kindness to Mara.
