# The Living Road narrative bible

## Purpose

Ashfall Road is told as a record of difficult movement through a world that remembers people. The wasteland is not a sequence of anonymous hazards. A working radio, a silenced alarm, a shared fire, or a repaired water line changes who survives long enough to meet the player again.

The voice is restrained second-person present tense. It observes concrete details before interpreting them. It never congratulates the player, explains a moral, or promises that kindness will be rewarded. Hope appears as work performed by frightened people, not as relief supplied by the narrator.

## Passage rules

- Event introductions contain 60–90 words. They establish place, a specific disturbance, and a decision whose risks can be understood without revealing the result.
- Outcomes contain 30–50 words. They identify the physical action, the immediate consequence, and one residue that follows the survivor onward.
- Choice labels remain short verbs or commitments. The prose carries nuance; the label carries intent.
- Damage appears through breath, balance, blood, heat, impact, or failing strength. Conditions appear through their symptoms. The result interface supplies the exact numbers.
- Gained objects are handled, packed, loaded, worn, or deliberately left. Lost objects are surrendered, broken, spent, stolen, or consumed.
- A successful choice may still feel costly. A failed choice must remain intelligible rather than arbitrary.
- A lethal route requires at least three concrete warnings: evidence of scale, evidence that ordinary protection has failed, and a final opportunity to withdraw.
- No passage refers to percentages, targets, modifiers, content IDs, flags, event slots, or the game engine.

## Prohibited filler

Do not use generic closers such as:

- “The road collects its price.”
- “The choice holds.”
- “The memory follows you.”
- “You gather yourself and continue.”
- “The situation turns at the wrong instant.”
- “For a moment, the road loosens its grip.”
- “The mistake announces itself.”
- “The sudden quiet feels larger than the fight.”

Do not replace these with lightly altered equivalents. An outcome must be impossible to move into a different event without becoming obviously wrong.

## Regional language

### Shattered Outskirts

Concrete, rain trapped in rooms, domestic objects, weeds, pharmacy dust, evacuation paint, sagging power lines, old traffic signals, voices carried between apartments. Human absence is intimate here: a table remains set, a bus route still has a timetable, a curtain moves where no household should remain.

Avoid treating every building as a corpse. The region should feel recently abandoned enough that the survivor still expects someone to answer.

### Salt Flats

White glare, distances without scale, wind-erased tracks, black wrecks, dry mouths, cooling metal after sunset, mirages, exposed movement, radio signals traveling farther than people. Human groups build authority from shade, water, fuel, and the ability to see travelers coming.

Silence is expansive rather than merely empty. Night brings cold, stars, and deceptive intimacy around small fires.

### Drowned Marches

Black reeds, drowned roofs, oil on water, insects falling silent together, wet wood, fungal light, chemical rain, rope ferries, stilt structures, submerged machinery. Sound arrives through water before it arrives through air.

The marsh preserves objects and secrets badly. Identification, burial, clean water, and safe passage become moral resources.

### Hollow Industrial Zone

Soot, hot iron, stale lubricant, contracting pipes, warning lights, numbered lockers, suspended chains, automated duties, chemical colors, machinery that continues without purpose. The region asks whether repair is service, theft, or the restoration of an authority that should remain dead.

People here speak in procedures because procedures once kept them alive.

### Glass Wastes

Fused ground, trapped shadows, doubled horizons, thin singing underfoot, stored heat, exposed radiation, burned foundations, reflections that confuse distance, pilgrimage marks. Descriptions must telegraph hazards through physical evidence rather than supernatural vagueness.

Faith in this region grows from surviving what should have killed everyone.

### Underrail

Brake dust, damp tile, standing water, signal lamps, old maps, warm windows, tunnel acoustics, failing ventilation, maintenance doors, cables under tension. Every enclosed space has been claimed by someone or something that learned its rhythms.

The Citadel is close enough to reorganize every conversation. Hope becomes a form of pressure.

## Recurring people

### The Vale family — Channel Nine

- Known initially: a parent, an older daughter named Elian, and a younger boy named Sable are trapped somewhere beyond the outer blocks.
- Wants: directions that do not pretend certainty; enough power to keep their receiver alive.
- Fears: following a confident stranger into an exposed route; becoming a story someone tells instead of people who arrive.
- Remembers: the survivor’s exact tone and whether they offered help, surveillance, or silence.
- Voice: practical domestic language—blankets, batteries, medicine, doors—used against landscapes too large for it.

### Dena Orr and the Quiet Column — Siren thread

- Known initially: Dena once maintained municipal warning towers and recognizes the Outskirts siren’s maintenance signature.
- Wants: to move a column of displaced families without giving raiders a sound to follow.
- Fears: that every warning system eventually becomes a beacon for predators.
- Remembers: whether the tower fell silent and who accepted the danger of climbing it.
- Voice: concise instructions that occasionally fracture into guilt.

### Lio Marr — Road Debt

- Known initially: Lio is related to one of the pharmacy bandits, but not necessarily loyal to the gang that person became.
- Wants: a truthful account and the ledger that shows which travelers the gang robbed, spared, recruited, or killed.
- Fears: discovering that grief has made him useful to the same violence.
- Remembers: whether the survivor fought, fled, or used fear as a weapon.
- Voice: controlled questions, names repeated carefully, anger expressed through precision.

### Iven Saye — Water Commons

- Known initially: Iven was a pump technician before the marsh settlements began treating clean water as territory.
- Wants: water governed as a shared obligation instead of private leverage.
- Fears: that a functioning filter will create a throne faster than it creates health.
- Remembers: whether the survivor repaired the line, stripped it, or left it failing.
- Voice: mechanical vocabulary joined to moral conclusions he dislikes having to make.

### Noma and the Nightfire Caravan

- Known initially: Noma travels with a loose caravan whose membership changes after every region.
- Wants: to prove that hospitality can remain a rule even when nobody can enforce it.
- Fears: being forced to choose which guest a shared fire no longer protects.
- Remembers: who sat within the firelight and who watched from the dark.
- Voice: stories told as inventories of people, meals, and promises; warmth without naivety.

## Consequence design

Every recurring thread supports three attitudes rather than a binary morality:

1. Solidarity: accept cost or risk for another person.
2. Control: help only while directing the terms and information.
3. Distance: preserve personal survival and refuse the obligation.

No attitude always wins. Solidarity creates debts and exposure. Control creates useful order and resentment. Distance preserves resources and allows other people to define the survivor through absence.

Returning characters must cite something observable from the earlier encounter: a phrase spoken over the radio, the absence of a siren, the account of a witness, clear water arriving downstream, or a place left empty beside a fire. They never announce that they remember “a choice.”

## Editorial checklist

- Could this introduction belong to another event after changing one noun? If yes, rewrite it.
- Does the outcome show the authored item, pressure, injury, or condition in the fiction?
- Does failure explain the error without insulting the player?
- Does success preserve uncertainty appropriate to the world?
- Does a returning person remember evidence rather than game state?
- Are danger warnings concrete and escalating?
- Is every sentence doing new work?
- Is the final line specific enough that it cannot become stock narration?
