# Part one — the Channel Nine arc, source to payoff


## outskirts_child_radio — Voice on Channel Nine   [compact, 63 words]

A handheld radio hangs from a traffic-light cable, repeating a child’s voice over Channel Nine. The message gives coordinates, asks for a person named Talia, and restarts whenever wind turns the set. Between repetitions, faint breathing occupies the carrier. Rooftops and power lines offer several possible origins. Answering may guide a family toward safety—or tell every receiver nearby that someone useful is listening.

**[0] Answer with calm instructions** — PRESENCE • 65% SUCCESS • NEED 8+

*success (41 words)* — A woman answers before your second instruction. Behind her voice, two children argue over a blanket. You guide Talia Vale toward the tarp market using landmarks she can confirm, and her relief steadies your own purpose after the frequency goes quiet.

*failure (34 words)* — The recorded child completes the message and begins again. No living speaker acknowledges your directions. You repeat them until uncertainty becomes embarrassment, then lower the radio with the voice still asking strangers to answer.

**[1] Triangulate the signal** — WITS • 45% SUCCESS • NEED 12+

*success (40 words)* — You compare signal strength beneath three power lines and locate the transmitter’s relay kit inside a maintenance cabinet. The family is elsewhere, but the compass and salvageable components reveal how their message traveled—and which bearing it was meant to follow.

*failure (41 words)* — Reflections from concrete and hanging cable make every bearing seem conclusive for a few minutes. You cross the same intersection twice before admitting the signal cannot be isolated here. The wasted hour leaves Channel Nine no clearer and your concentration frayed.

## lr_blue_van — The Blue Van   [substantial, 159 words]

A blue delivery van waits where the salt road divides, its windows curtained with pieces of a child's blanket, a crooked nine painted high across the rear doors.

The woman at the engine bay has the radio in her hand rather than above her head. She is not hunting a signal. She is waiting on one, and when she hears you on the salt she says the callsign from the outskirts out loud, testing it against your face the way you test a bearing against the sun.

The children are already out from under the chassis. The younger boy stands close enough to her leg to be pulled behind it; the older girl holds the paper map open with both thumbs, and there is a line drawn on it in pencil that follows the landmarks you named. It stops where your directions stopped. Everything past that is blank, and all three of them are looking at the blank part.

**[0] Repeat what you told them** — PRESENCE • 55% SUCCESS • NEED 10+

*success (105 words)* — You give the cadence back to her exactly: the same two notes, the same order of landmarks, the water tower before the culvert and not after it. She listens with her head turned slightly away, which is how people listen when they are checking rather than hearing.

Then she introduces herself. Talia Vale, and the children are Elian and Sable, and the boy comes out from under the chassis without being told. She takes one more direction from you and writes it on the paper map in pencil, beneath the last one, where it can be crossed out if it turns out to be wrong.

*failure (95 words)* — You give it back close but not exact. The culvert comes before the water tower, and the second note of the callsign sits a shade high, and both mistakes are small enough that a stranger would never catch them.

Talia Vale catches them. She does not accuse you of anything. She simply stops answering, closes the van doors while you are still explaining, and gets the engine on the third attempt. Through the blanket curtain you can see the girl writing. Your callsign goes onto the map beside a mark that is not a route.

**[1] Repair their receiver** — WITS • 35% SUCCESS • NEED 14+

*success (101 words)* — You clean salt from the contacts with the edge of your sleeve and bridge a burned trace with a length of wire, and the set comes back with the flat hiss of a channel that is open and empty. Talia does not thank you for it. She keys the handset once, hears the hiss answer her, and hands you a frequency written on the corner of the map.

One call at sunset, she says. If it goes unanswered twice, do not keep calling. The children have learned what an unanswered call means and she would rather they did not practise it.

*failure (93 words)* — The casing opens easily. The board underneath does not forgive being touched: one contact lifts with the corrosion still on it, there is a small blue spark, and Channel Nine stops existing.

Talia's anger stays quiet because the children are listening. She takes the dead set out of your hands, looks at it, and gives it back to you, which is worse than keeping it. They will travel east now with no voice ahead of them and no way to hear one coming. You are carrying the only radio any of you had.

**[2] Leave them your bearing** — CERTAIN

*outcome (92 words)* — You mark the safer line east on their paper map, the culvert road rather than the open pan, and you step back from the van before gratitude can turn into an arrangement.

Talia studies the route. Then she studies you, for longer, and decides not to ask the question she was assembling. The family keeps the information and none of your company. Walking away, you hear the engine catch behind you on the first try, which means she had it running before you arrived and chose to stand in the open anyway.

## lr_voice_in_reeds — A Voice in the Reeds   [substantial, 122 words]

Channel Nine comes out of the marsh in pieces. The voice is Elian's, the older Vale child, pitched low and close to the handset so the others will not hear her using the battery.

The van is off the raised road and buried to its axles beside a flooded service cut. Talia went for timber an hour ago. She has not come back, and Elian has stopped saying so out loud in case Sable is listening.

Through the reeds you can see the blue roof at an angle it should not sit at. You can also see a second trail, cut from the water toward the van, pushing the black stems aside in a line that does not close up behind it.

**[0] Follow Talia’s tracks** — WITS • 35% SUCCESS • NEED 14+

*success (108 words)* — The tracks divide twice and you take the newer edge both times, where the water has not yet finished filling the print. You find Talia waist-deep in a drainage cut, holding a plank above the surface with both arms locked, because something has been nosing the mud under her since she went in and she has worked out that the plank is the only part of her it has not tried.

You bring her backward by inches, along the firm edge, talking the whole way. When her boots find the bank she says your callsign once, and this time there is nothing in it that is checking you.

*failure (108 words)* — The tracks divide where the rain has filled them and you choose the older line, which is the wrong one, and you lose forty minutes proving it.

By the time you get back Talia has dragged herself out alone. One boot is gone, her leg is opened along the shin from something under the mud, and the fever is already bright in her eyes in a way that will not wait for the marsh to be behind them. She does not blame you; there is no breath spare for it. Elian watches you come back empty-handed from the direction you went, and does not say anything at all.

**[1] Free the van first** — STRENGTH • 60% SUCCESS • NEED 9+

*success (110 words)* — You get the jack plate under the axle and lever stone in behind each wheel, and you make the children push only in the moment the mud lets go, so that nothing is wasted and nobody is under the van when it moves.

It comes up onto the raised road with the sound of a cork leaving a bottle. Talia comes back through the reeds limping ten minutes later, sees the van on firm ground with both children on the far side of it, and understands in one look that you got the escape ready before you went looking for her. She hands you the bracket she broke getting free.

*failure (102 words)* — The wheel spins deep and throws poisoned mud in a fan across both children before you can call it off. You stop before the axle goes, but the sound has already crossed open water in every direction, and sound is the one thing this marsh carries better than it carries people.

Talia comes back at a run. Nobody argues. They take what small hands can carry and leave the van where it sits, blue roof at the wrong angle, doors open, curtains still pinned. Sable keeps turning round to look at it until the reeds close over the last of the paint.

**[2] Guide them out by radio** — CERTAIN

*outcome (103 words)* — You stay on the raised path and do it with your voice: unload the heavy side first, lay the boards under the downhill wheels, put your weight where the reeds stand up rather than where they lie flat. Elian relays each instruction to the others in a flat little voice that does not waste a word.

They are out inside the hour and they never see you. At sunset the channel opens and Talia says thank you, and then asks, carefully, that you keep doing it this way. Whatever is in the water followed the noise of the van and not the family.

## lr_last_battery — The Last Battery   [substantial, 130 words]

The radio in the factory break room is saying your callsign. Not calling it; saying it, in a queue, between evacuation notices, the way a name gets read off a list.

The Vale family is in the building across the yard. Between you and them is a security door on a failing circuit, and the only power in the room is the last battery, which is feeding the transmitter that is saying your name.

Elian counts the minutes it has left out loud, because counting is a job and she would rather have one. Behind her, Talia is arguing with a man who has offered the family passage east in exchange for the children's frequency. He is being reasonable about it. That is the part that makes your hands cold.

**[0] Open the security door** — WITS • 20% SUCCESS • NEED 17+

*success (108 words)* — You get the plate off the scorched controller and bridge the two contacts that still have metal on them, and the door lifts on a chain of reluctant teeth with maybe a minute of battery left.

There are more people behind it than there should be. The Vales are there, and three strangers the family took in at some point and did not mention, and Talia's first move is to put herself between you and them until she is certain which kind of arrival you are. Then she goes back inside and comes out with a waxed dust cloak folded flat, kept for the day they reached somewhere.

*failure (73 words)* — Your bridge takes. Then the frame takes it too, and current goes through the door and through your hands, and you come off the plate hard enough that the floor decides where you land.

The door welds itself shut in the closed position. You can hear them on the other side of it. You can hear Elian say your callsign once, checking, and then the battery goes and takes the voice with it.

**[1] Negotiate with the broker** — PRESENCE • 45% SUCCESS • NEED 12+

*success (79 words)* — You do not argue with the broker. You price him out loud, in front of the people he is selling to: what the frequency is worth, who he sells it to, what happens to a family whose channel is known. You keep your voice flat and you keep going until the arithmetic is finished.

The three strangers do the sum first. They open their side of the door themselves and the broker's arrangement stops existing without anybody touching him.

*failure (90 words)* — You name his price out loud and he takes it as an advertisement. Within a minute he is rebroadcasting your offer as proof that the family has friends worth having, and other voices start crowding onto the frequency to find out how much.

Talia cuts transmission before anyone can triangulate her. The last thing that crosses is her hand going to the switch. You are left with static, a yard, and the knowledge that you turned a family nobody could locate into a family several people are now looking for.

**[2] Tell them to save the battery** — CERTAIN

*outcome (101 words)* — You give Talia the eastern bearing, the culvert side of the rail cut, the water that can be trusted and the water that cannot. Then you tell her to shut the radio down and leave it down.

She does not thank you and she does not argue. The carrier drops mid-word. It preserves a few hours of power, and it removes the only thread by which either of you could ever find out what happened to the other. Elian stops counting. In the quiet afterward you notice you have been listening for a channel that is not going to open again.

## outskirts_bus — The Sealed Bus   [compact, 97 words]

An evacuation bus rests across two lanes, its tires melted into shallow black pools. Someone wired the rear doors together from inside and pressed strips of family photographs into the rubber seal. Soot blinds every window except one circle rubbed clear at a child’s height. Through it you can count what is stacked under the driver’s seat: two flat ration wrappers and the shoulder of a sealed bottle. The doors will only open by force. Through the same circle, torn paneling has exposed the emergency release cable, and a first-aid bracket hangs beside it, clips still shut.

**[0] Pry the door apart** — STRENGTH • 70% SUCCESS • NEED 7+

*success (40 words)* — The frame groans apart beneath steady pressure. Inside, seat belts bind empty blankets into the shapes of sleeping passengers. Two ration bars and a sealed bottle remain under the driver’s seat, overlooked when whoever wired the doors chose another exit.

*failure (43 words)* — The corroded frame releases all at once and hammers your shoulder against the step. Pain locks the joint before you can pull free. The doors remain crossed by wire, and every attempt to lift your arm confirms how badly the impact twisted it.

**[1] Trace the emergency release** — WITS • 55% SUCCESS • NEED 10+

*success (40 words)* — You follow the release cable beneath torn wall panels until your fingers find its manual catch. The doors fold inward without disturbing the photographs. A first-aid pouch yields clean bandages, and the cable bracket supplies metal small enough to carry.

*failure (41 words)* — The emergency circuit still holds one poisonous breath. When your tool bridges the terminals, the battery case splits and vents sweet metallic vapor into your face. You stagger outside with watering eyes, a burned throat, and contamination prickling across exposed skin.
